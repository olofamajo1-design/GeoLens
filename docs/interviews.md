## Analysis — separate evidence from interpretation

| Theme | What was reported | What we may tentatively infer (not validated) |
|---|---|---|
| News discovery | 5/5 mentioned social media in Q1 | GeoLens may need to be easy to discover/share through social channels. |
| Interest in prior context | P01 Iran background; P02 pre-October 7; P03 USSR/Russia–Ukraine; P04 US–Iran background; P05 El Niño history | Historical explanations could be useful, but P05 did not claim difficulty understanding most news. |
| Conflicting accounts | All five gave examples of differing accounts or uncertainty | Comparison of claims could help; examples do not establish any allegation is true. |
| Verification workarounds | P01/P02/P03 used Google or YouTube; P04 reported examining both sides; P05 described source/coherence checks | A connected source-and-context experience might reduce effort; no efficiency test yet. |
| Trust, misinformation and bias | P02 perceived Western-media bias; P03 questioned which channel to trust; P04 mentioned double standards; P05 mentioned propaganda | Trust is a topic to investigate, not proof of biased reporting by a specific publisher. |

## Instructor's reflection questions

**1. What surprised you? (proposed reflection; learner to confirm):** It was interesting that several readers described actively researching background and checking claims after seeing news on social media, rather than simply accepting the first story.

**2. What do you now believe differently? (proposed reflection; learner to confirm):** I originally thought the main problem might be understanding complicated news. The interviews suggest historical context, conflicting accounts and deciding which sources to trust may be equally important. One respondent said they usually understand news through follow-up, so not everyone needs simplified news.

**3. Which problem or complaint did at least two people mention?** P01–P04 expressly sought background on significant events; P05 named a prior-context example. All five mentioned some kind of conflicting reporting or claim. This is a pattern among interviewees, not a population estimate.
